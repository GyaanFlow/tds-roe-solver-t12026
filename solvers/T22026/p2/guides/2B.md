## 1 · TL;DR (30-second read)

An impact note claims a wind-stow scheduling pilot cut **DSM penalty by 31.6%** on a high-wind day, and should be considered for wider use.

The number is **real, but it overstates the pilot's effect.** Recomputing from `dispatch_blocks.csv`, both figures reproduce **exactly** (penalty **662,444 → 453,086 = −31.6%**; gap **379.0 → 282.8 MWh = −25.4%**). But 31.6% is a **cross-day** comparison — **28 May (conventional) vs 29 May (AI)** — and 28 May is the **single highest-penalty day** in the file (a *conventional* day, **4 Jun**, even settled *below* the AI day).

The data actually contains a **cleaner, same-day counterfactual the note ignored.** `base_schedule_mw` is the **pre-pilot QCA schedule**; the pilot revised only **12 of 96 blocks on 29 May** (lowering them). Holding **actual generation fixed** and recomputing 29 May on the base (no-pilot) schedule gives **Rs 549,664** vs **Rs 453,086** with the pilot — a **within-day effect of −Rs 96k ≈ −17.5%**, falling *entirely* on those 12 revised blocks. So the effect you can actually identify is **~17.5%, roughly half the advertised 31.6%.**

**Defensible verdict → *Credit the arithmetic, but report the ~17.5% within-day effect, not 31.6%.*** The 31.6% is the pilot vs the worst conventional day; the same-day, weather-matched counterfactual is ~17.5%. And even that is **one pilot day** — penalty-only, forecast-skill unproven — so the pilot's *general* effect stays unidentified. **Don't scale or publish "31.6%" on this.**

## 2 · Scenario in plain English

You are assessing whether the evidence supports an impact note's headline: the wind-stow-aware scheduling pilot **reduced DSM penalty by 31.6%.** You must state the **strongest conclusion you can defend** *and* **what the evidence cannot establish** — and you're told to **recompute what is reproducible from the block data** and **not** to assume a favourable comparison is causal.

Why it matters: a clean-looking percentage from a **single day** is exactly the kind of result that gets a pilot promoted. But a **DSM penalty** is a *banded* deviation charge computed block-by-block, and a one-day-vs-one-day comparison mixes the **method** with the **weather**, the **schedule**, and the **choice of comparison day.** Your job is to separate the arithmetic (which is fine) from the **comparison it rests on** — a cross-day, cherry-picked baseline — to surface the **cleaner same-day counterfactual already sitting in the block data** (`base_schedule_mw`), and to name the measurement that *would* settle the rest.

## 5 · Verified data findings (the frozen analysis — cite these)

**1. Both headline numbers reproduce exactly.**
From the block data: 28 May submitted **4507.3** MWh / generated **4196.5**; 29 May submitted **4455.2** / generated **4231.1** — matching the note. Daily `dsm_penalty_rs` sums to **662,444 (28 May) → 453,086 (29 May) = −31.6%.** The note's "generation gap" (**379.0 → 282.8**, −25.4%) is the **sum of absolute block deviations** (net schedule−generation is 310.7 → 224.1). Recomputing penalty from the band table independently lands within **0.02%** of the column. *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv` + `AI_Pilot_Impact_Note.md`)*

**2. A cleaner, same-day counterfactual exists — and it says ~17.5%, not 31.6%.**
The note compares two *different days*, but the file already carries a no-pilot schedule for the pilot day. `base_schedule_mw` is the **pre-pilot QCA schedule**; it is **identical to `submitted_schedule_mw` on all 84 non-pilot blocks** and differs **only on the 12 `pilot_revision` blocks (all 29 May)**, which the pilot **lowered by ~18.9 MW on average.** Holding **actual generation fixed** and recomputing the 29 May penalty on the base schedule (no pilot) vs the submitted schedule (pilot) gives **Rs 549,664 → Rs 453,086 = −Rs 96,476 ≈ −17.5%** — and the *entire* difference falls on those 12 blocks (non-pilot blocks contribute exactly 0). This is the **within-day, same-weather, same-generation** effect of the revision, and it is **about half the advertised 31.6%.** Caveats: it is **penalty-only** (not net value), it assumes the pilot changed only the *schedule* (not physical generation), and it can't prove the revision was **ex-ante forecast skill** rather than hindsight. *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv`)*

**3. The 31.6% is a cross-day comparison against a cherry-picked — and firmware-confounded — baseline.**
28 May is the **single highest-penalty day** in the 8-day file (Rs 662,444). Two other high-wind days ran **conventional**: **3 Jun = Rs 497,360** and **4 Jun = Rs 403,391** — and **4 Jun (conventional) settled *below* the AI day (Rs 453,086).** That's the single strongest counter-point, but note a confound: **all May days ran firmware 4.7, all June days firmware 4.8**, so the 4-Jun comparison mixes firmware with method. The clean point survives without it: comparing the pilot to the **worst** conventional day inflates the apparent gain (31.6%) well beyond the **within-day** effect (~17.5%). *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv`)*

**4. For *generality* it is still one pilot day (n = 1).**
`schedule_source = pilot_revision` appears on **only 12 of 96 blocks, all on 29 May** — that single day is the *entire* pilot dataset. The same-day counterfactual (finding 2) identifies the effect **on that day**; it cannot establish the effect across typical high-wind days, its **stability or sign**, or its **net-value** impact. The note itself concedes high-wind stow days are **infrequent.** *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv`)*

**5. The penalty is a banded step-function; ~10 STOW blocks carry ~96–98% of a day's rupees.**
Deviation is charged by band (>15% → Rs 2.0/kWh). On **both** 28 and 29 May exactly **10 blocks** are in `STOW` and land in the **Rs 2.0** band; those blocks carry **98.0% (28 May) / 96.5% (29 May)** of the day's penalty. File-wide, `STOW` is only **4.6% of blocks but 92.8% of penalty rupees** — the charge lives almost entirely in a handful of stow blocks. The pilot's gain is the **smaller deviation magnitude** in those blocks after the 12-block revision lowered the submitted schedule ahead of stow. *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv` + `DSM_Commercial_Extract.pdf`)*

**6. 28 & 29 May are weather- *and* firmware-matched — which is what makes the same-day counterfactual clean.**
28 vs 29 May: wind mean **4.8 / 4.8** m/s, gust max **13.1 / 13.4**, cloud **1.0 / 1.0**, availability **98.5% / 98.5%**, curtailment **0 / 0**, firmware **4.7 / 4.7**, **10 / 10** stow blocks. Because the two days match (and finding 2's counterfactual holds *within* 29 May), the ~17.5% estimate isn't confounded by weather or firmware — unlike the 4-Jun point. But a matched pair is still **one pilot day** for the generality question. *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv`)*

**7. DSM penalty ≠ full commercial impact.**
The extract prices energy at **Rs 2.72/kWh** and notes delivered energy stays valuable "except where separately curtailed." The pilot **lowered the submitted schedule** to shrink the deviation penalty — but committing to deliver less may **forgo scheduled-energy value**, so a −17.5% *penalty* cut is **not** a −17.5% *commercial* gain. The honest metric is **net commercial value (energy value − penalty)** across many days — which the note doesn't compute. *(source: `DSM_Commercial_Extract.pdf`)*

## 7 · Traps that lose marks here

> [!CAUTION]
> - **Treating 31.6% as the pilot's effect.** It's a **cross-day** comparison (28 vs 29 May); the same-day counterfactual is **~17.5%**. Reproduce the 31.6%, then report the cleaner number.
> - **Claiming "no counterfactual exists."** It does — `base_schedule_mw` is the same-day, no-pilot schedule. Recompute 29 May on it (holding actual fixed): **≈ −17.5% (−Rs 96k)**, all on the 12 revised blocks. Waving at "n=1" while ignoring this leaves easy calibration marks on the table.
> - **Not recomputing.** The rubric asks you to; doing so reveals the note's "gap" is the *absolute-deviation sum* (not schedule−generation) and that the penalty is band-driven.
> - **Missing the cherry-picked baseline.** 28 May is the worst day; a conventional day (4 Jun) beat the AI day — **but** 4 Jun ran firmware 4.8 vs 4.7, so cite it *with* that caveat, not as clean proof.
> - **Ignoring the step-function.** Penalty jumps at band edges (>15% → Rs 2.0/kWh); ~10 STOW blocks carry **96–98%** of a day's rupees, so small deviation changes there swing everything.
> - **Judging penalty alone.** Lowering the schedule cuts the penalty but may forgo energy value; net value = energy (Rs 2.72/kWh) − penalty. A one-metric view is partial.
> - **Over-swinging to "it's fake."** The number is real and the mechanism is plausible — the defensible read is *"reproduced, but the effect is ~17.5% and only shown for one day,"* not *"debunked."*
> - **Forgetting the generality gap.** Even with a clean within-day estimate, it's **one pilot day** — you can't yet estimate the effect across high-wind days or prove the revision was ex-ante forecast, not hindsight.

### 8b · Rejected hypotheses + causal limits (→ `## Rejected Hypotheses and Causal Limits`)

- **"The 31.6% is fabricated/wrong"** → **rejected:** it reproduces to the rupee from block data.
- **"The AI method *caused* a 31.6% reduction"** → **overstated:** 31.6% is the cross-day comparison against the worst conventional day; the **same-day** counterfactual (`base` vs `submitted`, actual fixed) is **−17.5%**.
- **"No counterfactual is possible — it's fully unidentified"** → **rejected:** `base_schedule_mw` is the same-day, weather-matched, no-pilot schedule; the **within-day effect (~17.5%) IS identifiable.**
- **"Lower penalty = better commercial outcome"** → **unproven:** penalty is one part of settlement; net value (energy − penalty) isn't computed.
- **Still causally unidentified:** the effect's **generality** across typical high-wind days and its **sign/stability** (only n=1 pilot day); whether the revision was **ex-ante forecast skill vs hindsight**; and its **net-value** impact after energy value is netted.

### 8c · Next measurement / validation design (→ `## Next Measurement`, pick one)

- **Already partly done — extend it:** the same-day counterfactual (`base` vs `submitted`, actual fixed) is computable *now* and gives ~17.5% on 29 May. The gap is **repetition**: run the AI schedule (with its base shadow) over **≥N high-wind stow days** and report the distribution of within-day effects, not one ratio.
- **Hold firmware constant** so the comparator isn't confounded (the 4.7→4.8 split currently aligns with May→June).
- **Report net commercial value (energy value − DSM penalty)**, not penalty alone — lowering the schedule can forgo energy value.
- **Establish the revision was ex-ante:** confirm the pilot lowered the schedule *before* generation was known (genuine forecast skill), not after (hindsight).
