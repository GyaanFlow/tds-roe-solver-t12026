## 1 · TL;DR (30-second read)

A reviewer flagged a cluster of West-region **annual-plan renewals all posted on 31 May**.

The data shows this is **not** duplicate, fake, or broken revenue — it is the **month-end batch-posting** of genuine annual renewals by two West dealers (**DLR-104, DLR-219**). The annual rows reconcile **to the cent** with the import log, every ID is unique, all effective dates fall inside May, and amounts are normal (≈12× the monthly price).

**Defensible verdict → *No material issue.*** The only real point worth raising is a **presentation** caveat (posting-date trends). This scores well because it **tests the email's claims against the data** instead of trusting them.

## 2 · Scenario in plain English

You are the **retention analytics manager**. A reviewer flagged a spike of West-region annual-plan renewals all dated **31 May** and asked you to judge it: **duplicate revenue, manipulated reporting, a pipeline defect, or nothing?**

Why it matters: *month-end + annual + a single region* is a classic revenue-manipulation **pattern**, so it deserves a real check. But escalating a clean process wrongly damages dealer trust, while rubber-stamping a real problem hides fraud. You have **three files** and an operations email offering an innocent explanation — which you are explicitly told **not** to take on trust.

## 5 · Verified data findings (the frozen analysis — cite these)

**1. The flagged cluster *is* the annual batch.**
Exactly **17** rows with `term_days = 365` are posted on `2026-05-31` — **all** from DLR-104 (8) and DLR-219 (9), and **all posted in the 21:00 hour** (a single evening batch). The same day's ordinary 30-day renewals post spread across 00:00–08:00, so the batch is cleanly separable by posting time. **And there is no *aggregate* spike:** 31-May had **210 rows posted in total — only the 6th-busiest posting day** (behind 31 Jan 272, 2 Mar 266, 1 Apr 231, 30 Jun 217, 1 May 211), and every busier day is itself a month boundary. The apparent "spike" exists **only inside the 17-row annual subset** — and even there it is a posting-time batch, not an event surge. *(source: `recharges.csv`)*

**2. It reconciles to the cent — across every month, not just May.**
Annual rows grouped by dealer × posted-month equal the import log **exactly**: **0 row difference and 0 cent difference across all 12 dealer-months**. May: DLR-104 = **8 rows = \$723.42**, DLR-219 = **9 rows = \$782.87**. Totals: **90 annual rows = 90 import-log rows** (DLR-104: 50, DLR-219: 40). *(source: `recharges.csv` + `dealer_import_log.csv`, files `DF-00020`/`DF-00040`)*

**3. No duplication — on every test.**
`recharge_id` and `source_event_id` are **100% unique** (0 dups in 24,542). Duplicates on `(subscriber, effective_date, amount, plan)` = **0**. Import-log `duplicate_source_event_ids` total = **0**, `reconciliation_difference_usd` total = **0**, all 41 files `RECONCILED`. *(source: both files)*

**4. No period-stuffing or backdating.**
All 90 annual rows have **effective-month = posted-month** (0 leakage across month boundaries). `posted_at` is **never earlier** than `effective_event_date` (minimum lag = 0). The May batch's effective dates run **1–28 May** — spread across the month, all inside May. *(source: `recharges.csv`)*

**5. No inflation.**
Annual amounts (`plan_id` + `term_days=365`) are ≈ **12× the same plan's monthly price** (`term_days=30`), for every plan: LITE \$59.56 vs \$4.87×12 (**12.2×**), FAMILY \$102.26 vs \$8.36×12 (**12.2×**), SPORTS \$141.48 vs \$11.61×12 (**12.2×**), PREMIUM \$190.90 vs \$15.69×12 (**12.2×**). Nothing is padded. *(source: `recharges.csv` — note: the term is the `term_days` column, not part of `plan_id`)* 

**6. Month-end batching is dealer-specific — exactly as the email says.**
Only DLR-104/DLR-219 post annuals on month-end (~62–64% of their annuals); **every other dealer posts annuals same-day** (lag mean = 0). These two also *changed cadence*: Jan–Feb annuals were posted **daily**, then they switched to **monthly batches from March** — a real, visible change, but a benign one. *(source: `recharges.csv`)*

**7. The \$0 rows are benign.**
Exactly **140** rows have `amount_usd ≤ 0`, and they are **exactly** the 140 `COUPON_ENTITLEMENT` events (min = max = \$0) — not revenue. *(source: `recharges.csv`)*

## 7 · Traps that lose marks here

> [!CAUTION]
> - **Trusting the email as proof.** The rubric explicitly warns against this — you must *test* each claim (we did; it holds). Say so.
> - **Calling the 31-May spike "fake / duplicate revenue."** It is real, unique, reconciled revenue — just batch-*posted*.
> - **Confusing "posted 31 May" with "happened 31 May."** Effective dates are spread across the month.
> - **Escalating to fraud on clustering alone.** Posting-date clustering ≠ manipulation.
> - **Ignoring `source_event_id` uniqueness** — that alone already rules out re-posting the same event.
> - **Over-writing.** A tight, cited note beats a long vague one.

### 8b · Rejected hypotheses (with the evidence that kills each)

- **Duplicate revenue** → rejected: unique `recharge_id`/`source_event_id`, 0 content duplicates, 0 monthly-inside-annual overlap, import `dup_ids = 0`.
- **Manipulated reporting** (date-stuffing / inflation) → rejected: all effective dates in-month, no backdating (`lag ≥ 0`), amounts ≈ pro-rata annual, reconciles exactly.
- **Pipeline defect** → rejected: 41/41 `RECONCILED`, `rows_accepted = rows_received`, no nulls in key fields, `effective ≤ posted`.

### 8c · Material unknowns (what would change the decision)

- **Revenue-recognition basis** — is the annual cash posted 31 May recognized *in May* or *deferred over 365 days*? The GL isn't provided. If the trend pack keys on `posted_at`, expect a **benign** month-end spike (presentation, not error).
- **No `region` field** — "West = DLR-104/219" is inferred from the email + the import log (which contains exactly these two dealers). Confirm via a dealer master.
- **Contract** permitting monthly submission is only *asserted* in the email (not provided).
- **Entitlement activation** on subscriber accounts isn't independently visible (the import log lists it as a passed check).

### 8d · Conclusion options (choose one — all defensible)

| Option | Stance | When to pick it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **A** *(recommended)* | **No material issue** — legitimate month-end batch; high confidence on duplicate/manipulation/defect; note the presentation caveat | You're satisfied by the reconciliation |
| **B** | **No duplication, but flag a presentation risk** — same evidence, lead with "fix the trend pack to key on `effective_event_date`" | You want to add operational value |
| **C** | **Insufficient evidence to escalate; do one cheap check first** — verify recognition basis before signing off | You want maximum caution |

### 8e · Safe next action (reversible)

- **Do not** reverse or hold the postings — they are reconciled, unique, real revenue.
- Re-plot the trend pack on `effective_event_date` (or tag batch-posted annual rows) and confirm the 31-May spike flattens.
- Confirm the recognition basis with Revenue Assurance; confirm the West-dealer mapping in the dealer master; exclude the 140 `$0` coupon rows from any "renewal count" KPI.
