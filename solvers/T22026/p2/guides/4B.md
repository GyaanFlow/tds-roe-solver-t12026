## 1 · TL;DR (30-second read)

A new global spare-parts search is *"much better at finding matches"* across the 4 plants. A planner has **24 open external purchase requests** and asks: which of these can we kill by pulling stock from another site instead of buying? The maintenance lead (Leon Dube) has explicitly warned: **"do not turn every hit into a transfer recommendation."**

**The whole case is a calibration/triage test.** The wrong answer is *"the search found matches, so transfer them."* The right answer sorts the 24 into three disciplined buckets using the policy's own gates — **manufacturer part number → revision → qualification → truly-free stock**:

- **Actionable now — 8 requests, $20,536 (12.3%).** Part number present **and** a single other site has free, revision-matching, `QUALIFIED_COMMON` stock ≥ qty.
- **Needs check — 12 requests, $64,234 (38.6%).** A candidate exists but **one specific thing** is unconfirmed: identity (blank part number), a revision gap, a `SITE_SPECIFIC_CHECK`, an availability **lag**, or a split-across-sites quantity.
- **Not transferable — 4 requests, $81,725 (49.1%).** All four are the **$18k `MTR-4401` servo drive**, whose only other-site stock is **reserved for a safety-critical asset** — the exact case Engineering already declined.

**Defensible verdict → *Transfer only the 8; return the 12 with a named check each; keep buying the 4.*** The headline saving is **$20.5k firm**, not the $166.5k the raw search seems to offer. (Note the total is **qty × unit quote = $166,495.60**, not the $152,558 you get by summing the quote column — see §5.)

## 2 · Scenario in plain English

You are a **maintenance planner** at Aurelia Consumer Products (ACP), which runs four plants — **Riverbend, Harbor, Northport, Meridian**. Buying a spare externally is slow and expensive; if an identical, free spare sits at a sister plant, you should transfer it instead. A new **global catalog search** now surfaces cross-site matches well (it even recognises spelling variants like "servo drive / servo-drive / S/D").

The risk cuts both ways. **Miss** a real internal match → you waste money on an external buy. **Trust** a bad match → you ship the wrong revision, raid a spare that was reserved for a safety-critical machine, or chase stock that isn't actually free. Leon Dube's email is the calibration key: **part number is the first identity check**, then revision / UOM / qualification / reservation, and **"global available quantity can lag a local reservation."** Your job is not to build the search — it's to **triage its 24 hits into transfer / check / buy**, and defend the split.

## 5 · Verified data findings (the frozen analysis — cite these)

**1. The full triage: 8 / 12 / 4 — and the money is not where the count is.**
The 24 open buys are worth **$166,495.60** — that is **qty × `external_quote_usd`**, because the quote is a **unit** price, not a line total (finding 1b). **Actionable now: 8 requests = $20,536 (12.3 %). Needs check: 12 = $64,234 (38.6 %). Not transferable: 4 = $81,725 (49.1 %).** So roughly half the *value* is untouchable and only ~12 % is a clean win — the opposite of "the search will save us $166k." *(source: `part_requests.csv` + `spare_parts.csv` + `part_restrictions.csv`, recomputed)*

**1b. `external_quote_usd` is a *unit* price — so summing the column undercounts.**
On the **13** requests that carry a part number, `external_quote_usd ÷` the stock `unit_cost_usd` is **1.063–1.232** (a plausible external mark-up). If the quote were a *line* total, the four part-numbered qty-2 rows (PR-260702/703/707/710) would imply buying at ~**0.55×** catalog — impossible. So avoidable value is **qty × quote = $166,495.60**, not the column sum **$152,558.03** — summing the column silently treats every qty-2 line as qty-1 and **undercounts by $13,937.57**. Five requests are qty 2 (PR-260702/703/707/709/710); the rest qty 1. *(source: `part_requests.csv` × `spare_parts.csv`, recomputed)*

**2. Every "not transferable" request is the same $18k servo drive — and it's reserved.**
All **4** not-transferable rows (PR-260700/704/712/715) are the 2.2 kW servo drive **`MTR-4401`** — though only **PR-260712 actually spells the part number out**; **700/704/715 leave `manufacturer_part_no` blank**, and "servo drv 2.2 kW" matches MTR-4401 and *nothing else* in the catalog. The only cross-site stock is `RI-0001` (rev B, **reserved-for-critical**) and `ME-0003` (rev A, **reserved-for-critical**); `HA-0002` is rev A but **empty** (on-hand 0). So there is **no free stock to pull even after identity is confirmed** — here the binding constraint is the *reservation*, not the part number. This is *exactly* Leon's example — "Harbor had the exact drive… reserved against a safety-critical filler and Engineering declined." **Do not recommend pulling these.** *(source: `spare_parts.csv` + `part_restrictions.csv` + `maintenance_email.txt`)*

**3. Nearly half the requests can't be actioned because identity is unconfirmed.**
**11 of 24** requests (**$98,371** at qty × quote) have a **blank `manufacturer_part_no`**. The policy makes part number the **first** identity check, so these can't be auto-transferred even when a clean stock match exists. **Eight** of them drop to **needs check** ("confirm the part number, then transfer" — clean stock is waiting). The **other three are the blank servo requests (700/704/715)**, which stay **not transferable**: their only conceivable match (`MTR-4401`) is reserved/empty, so confirming identity changes nothing. *(source: `part_requests.csv` + `spares_transfer_policy.md`)*

**4. The availability-lag trap: a negative "available" is not proof of no stock.**
Two stock rows show `available_qty_global ≤ 0` **with positive on-hand**: `HA-0022` belt (**on-hand 3, available −1**) and `ME-0006` pump seal (**on-hand 1, available −1**). Leon: *"global available quantity can lag a local reservation."* So `−1` is a **flag to verify**, not a "no." This is why belt requests PR-260702 & PR-260707 are **needs check**, not "not transferable." *(source: `spare_parts.csv` + `maintenance_email.txt`)*

**5. The revision gap on the aseptic valve — and only two free units for four units of demand.**
**All** `VLV-3722` stock is **revision B** (NO-0011, HA-0012, ME-0013). But **PR-260705 and PR-260720 request revision A** → a real engineering question (confirm interchangeability), not an auto-match. And even for the rev-B requests, the **free** rev-B stock is only **2 units** — NO-0011 (×1) + ME-0013 (×1), both `QUALIFIED_COMMON` and unreserved; HA-0012's 2 units are `SITE_SPECIFIC_CHECK` **and** reserved-for-critical, so *not* free. Rev-B valve **demand** is **4 units** (PR-260703 qty 2 + PR-260713 + PR-260723) → at most **half** can ever be sourced internally, even after every check clears. So don't cite NO-0011/ME-0013 as the source for PR-703 *and* 713 *and* 723 — free stock is allocated **once**. *(source: `spare_parts.csv` + `part_restrictions.csv` + `part_requests.csv`)*

**6. Qualification gate: `SITE_SPECIFIC_CHECK` ≠ drop-in.**
Several free rows are `SITE_SPECIFIC_CHECK` (filters `NO-0025`, `ME-0027`; sensor `HA-0009`; valve `HA-0012`; bearing `NO-0015`). MT-18 requires receiving-site qualification, so these need a qualification confirmation before transfer even when the part number and revision match. *(source: `part_restrictions.csv` + `spares_transfer_policy.md`)*

**7. The 8 clean wins — what "actionable now" actually looks like.**
Each has **part number present + a single other site with free (positive-available, unreserved), revision-matching, `QUALIFIED_COMMON` stock ≥ qty**: HEPA filters PR-260701/706/708 (Harbor HA-0026, 3 free), bearing PR-260710 (Meridian, 4 free), remote-IO PR-260711 (Meridian, 4 free), belt PR-260718 (Meridian, 1 free), pump seals PR-260721/722 (Riverbend, 3 free). These are the safe cancellations. *(source: all three CSVs)*

**8. The global stock view is a month stale — so every "free" count needs a live re-check.**
`source_freshness.csv` dates the global CMMS snapshot (`MaintStar-global`) at **2026-06-26**, refreshed only **monthly**, while the request export (`ProcureNet`) is **2026-07-24** — the availability figures you triage on are **~4 weeks older** than the requests themselves, and the note adds *"local CMMS screens are live."* This is the plausible mechanism behind the `available_qty_global = −1` values, and it means even the 8 "actionable" transfers should be confirmed against **live local stock** before a PO is cancelled: a stale global view can show free stock a site has since consumed. *(source: `source_freshness.csv`)*

## 7 · Traps that lose marks here

> [!CAUTION]
> - **Turning every search hit into a transfer** — the single thing Leon's email tells you not to do. Recommending all 24 (or "most") transfers fails the calibration.
> - **Reading `available_qty_global = −1` as zero.** On-hand is positive; it's a lag. Flag it, don't kill the candidate.
> - **Ignoring the blank part number** on 11 requests and treating a description match as identity.
> - **Missing the valve revision mismatch** (rev A requested, only rev B stocked).
> - **Recommending the reserved servo drives** — high-value, reserved-for-critical, already declined once.
> - **Counting split-across-sites quantity as a single pull** (e.g. valve qty 2 = 1 + 1 at two sites) without flagging the consolidation / last-unit issue.
> - **Quoting $166k of "savings" as if it were all avoidable.** The defensible firm number is **$20.5k**; be explicit that the rest is conditional or unavailable — and note the raw figure is **$166.5k = qty × unit quote**, *not* the $152.6k you get by summing the quote column (that undercount is itself a trap).
