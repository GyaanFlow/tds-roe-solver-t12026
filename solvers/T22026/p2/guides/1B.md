## 1 · TL;DR (30-second read)

South-region complaint **cases** fell after the **NovaIVR (NOVA-S1)** pilot, and an ops email cites "transfers down" and a "containment dashboard above target" to argue self-service improved CX and should go national.

The data says the decline is **mostly a measurement + authentication artifact, not proven CX improvement.** Under NOVA-S1, **pre-authentication failures tripled** (24% of sessions vs ~8%), and the email itself confirms those sessions are **excluded from CareDesk case reporting** — so complaints "went quiet" partly because failed contacts **never become cases.** Meanwhile **outages did not fall**, so fewer complaints ≠ fewer problems. *Some* deflection is genuine (real new self-service for balance/pack lookups), so don't over-swing.

**Defensible verdict → *Not clean evidence of CX improvement; do NOT expand nationally yet.*** Fix the auth defects, add an all-contact denominator + repeat-contact metric, then re-measure. This scores well because it **tests the email's claims against the data** and **separates real gains from artifacts.**

## 2 · Scenario in plain English

You are a **forward-deployed analyst embedded with Customer Care.** After the **NovaIVR pilot** launched in the **South** region, the number of complaint **cases** in South dropped. Leadership wants you to judge: **is this evidence that self-service genuinely improved the customer experience, and should the pilot expand nationally?**

Why it matters: "cases went down" is the kind of number that gets a pilot promoted company-wide. But a **case** is only created when a contact reaches a certain point in the funnel — so "fewer cases" can mean *"fewer problems"* **or** *"more problems that never got logged."* The email offers the flattering reading and explicitly tells you the containment dashboard **ignores sessions that end before authentication.** You have three data files plus that email, and you must decide whether the drop is real CX improvement, a counting artifact, or a mix — and what one more conversation would settle it.

## 5 · Verified data findings (the frozen analysis — cite these)

**1. The pilot is South-only and started mid-May.**
`NOVA-S1` first appears **2026-05-10** and **only in South**. The other three regions (E/N/W) are **100% LEGACY** across all six months. So South after ~10 May is the only place anything changed — clean, but it also means **all comparisons must control for region and month.** *(source: `ivr_interactions.jsonl`, `pilot_version × region × month`)*

**2. The "quiet" is real, South-specific, and lands in June (the first full pilot month).**
South monthly cases: Jan 175 → Feb 166 → Mar 198 → Apr 184 → May 157 → **Jun 104**. Indexed to January, June South = **59**, while the control regions are flat the same month: **E 101, N 101, W 96.** The drop is genuine and localized to South — it is not a company-wide seasonal dip. *(source: `tickets.csv`)*

**3. South IVR session volume jumped ~12× — a logging regime change.**
South IVR sessions/month: **~88–110** under LEGACY (Jan–Apr) → **1,192 in May** (1,150 NOVA-S1 + 42 residual LEGACY) → **1,559 in June** (all NOVA-S1). NOVA records **every attempt** (including pre-auth drop-offs) where LEGACY logged far fewer. **You cannot compare LEGACY and NOVA rates as like-for-like without accounting for this.** *(source: `ivr_interactions.jsonl`)*

**4. Pre-authentication failures roughly *tripled* under NOVA.**
Sessions ending at the `authenticate` stage (the identification gate): **24.0% under NOVA-S1** vs **7.9% South-LEGACY** and **6.9% other regions.** Overall `ERROR` 18.7% vs 11.5%; `ABANDONED` 21.8% vs 12.9%. Auth/prompt error codes (`TOKEN_MISMATCH`, `ANI_LOOKUP_TIMEOUT`, `PIN_RETRY_LIMIT`, `PROMPT_TIMEOUT`) hit **13.4%** of NOVA sessions (the three pure-auth codes alone = 13.1%) — matching the email's own "authentication and prompt-timing items." *(source: `ivr_interactions.jsonl`)*

**5. Those pre-auth failures never become cases — the deflection.**
**650** NOVA sessions ended at `authenticate` with **no `case_id`** (302 ERROR, 162 ABANDONED, 186 RESOLVED); **464** of those failed outright (ERROR/ABANDONED). Widen it and **1,094** NOVA sessions ended ERROR/ABANDONED with **no case at all**. In **June alone, 382** pre-auth sessions produced no case. The email confirms these are counted in a **technical report, not CareDesk** — so they are invisible in the "complaints" number. **Be precise: these are sessions, not people — and it cuts both ways.** The 464 pre-auth failures come from **411 distinct subscribers**; **236 later got through** in a subsequent NOVA session or had a case created, but **175 were never seen again in NOVA.** So the defensible "lost-customer" floor is **~175**, not 464 — yet even the recovered ones paid a retry tax (**811 of 1,420 NOVA subscribers had >1 session, up to 8**). The suppression is real; just don't equate one session with one lost customer. *(source: `ivr_interactions.jsonl` + `email-ivr-pilot.eml`)*

**6. The containment dashboard is inflated by its own denominator.**
By the email's definition it "starts after a subscriber has been identified" — i.e. it **excludes pre-auth sessions.** Measured that way, NOVA containment = **64.8%** vs LEGACY **28.3%** (looks like a huge win). Put **all** sessions in the denominator and it's **56.1% vs 26.0%.** NOVA drops **24%** of sessions from the count vs **7.9%** under LEGACY — so "above target" partly reflects *who is counted*, not only *who is helped*. *(source: `ivr_interactions.jsonl`)*

**7. "Transfers are down" is literally true — but it *is* the deflection mechanism.**
The case-create → agent-transfer path fell from **49.5% of sessions (LEGACY) to 3.3% (NOVA)** (count 219 → 90). Under LEGACY, **half** of IVR sessions created a case and reached an agent; under NOVA almost none do. Fewer transfers means fewer paths by which an unresolved issue becomes a **logged complaint** — the metric moves in the flattering direction for an unflattering reason. *(source: `ivr_interactions.jsonl`)*

**8. Be fair: *some* deflection is genuine self-service.**
NOVA adds real self-service stages that **don't exist** in LEGACY: `balance_check` (29.1% of sessions) and `pack_info` (21.3%) — both **0%** under LEGACY. Simple balance/pack intents can now resolve without an agent, which plausibly explains part of the drop in **AGENT_DIRECT** (South 69→34) and **APP** (43→19) cases. Don't over-swing to "it's all fake" — the honest read is **mixed**. *(source: `ivr_interactions.jsonl` + `tickets.csv` origin × month)*

**9. The underlying problem rate did *not* fall — the confounder is killed.**
South outages stayed steady/high through the pilot: **May 10 events / 9,409 accounts, June 8 / 7,045** — comparable to Jan–Apr. Yet `NO_SIGNAL` complaints (which outages cause) fell **51→28** (May→June). **Fewer complaints with unchanged outages** points to suppression, not resolution — and the June deflection volume (**382** lost sessions) dwarfs the **~80-case** shortfall vs trend. *(source: `service_events.csv` + `tickets.csv`)*

## 6 · Claim vs. Reality

| The email / dashboard says | What the data actually shows | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| "Agent transfers are down" | True: 49.5% → 3.3%. But transfers are the path to a logged case; their collapse + tripled pre-auth deaths = deflection | **True but misleading** |
| "Case-containment dashboard above target" | Dashboard excludes ~24% of sessions (pre-auth); all-contact containment is 56%, not 65% | **Inflated by denominator** |
| (Implied) self-service improved CX → cases fell because problems were solved | Outages unchanged; pre-auth failures tripled; 464–1,094 sessions (411 distinct subs, ~175 never recovered) dropped with no case | **Not supported** |
| (Implied) safe to expand nationally | South-only pilot, open auth/prompt defects, no repeat-contact data | **Premature** |
| "Pre-auth sessions live in the tech report, not CareDesk" (Farah) | Confirmed by the data — and this is *exactly why* CareDesk went quiet | **Corroborated — it's the artifact** |
| New self-service genuinely helps some callers | Confirmed: `balance_check`/`pack_info` stages are new and resolve simple intents | **Partly real** |

## 7 · Traps that lose marks here

> [!CAUTION]
> - **Reading "fewer cases" as "happier customers."** This is survivorship — the count only sees contacts that got *through* the funnel.
> - **Trusting the containment dashboard without asking its denominator.** By design it excludes pre-auth failures; that's the whole trick.
> - **Ignoring the 12× session-volume jump.** LEGACY vs NOVA rates are not like-for-like until you account for the logging change.
> - **Blaming/crediting the IVR for the whole drop** while `service_events` (the real driver of `NO_SIGNAL`) stayed flat.
> - **Over-swinging to "it's all fake."** Some self-service deflection is genuine — the defensible read is *mixed*, and saying so scores better.
> - **Comparing South-NOVA only to South-LEGACY** and forgetting the E/N/W control and the March seasonal peak.
> - **Choosing Dev Khanna (Billing).** Wrong domain — this is a containment/case-definition question, which is **Farah's** area.
> - **Asking the interviewee "is my theory correct?"** The bios say ask for **definitions, records, examples, decisions** — not validation.

### 8b · Rejected hypotheses (with the evidence that kills each)

- **"Self-service genuinely cut complaints across the board"** → rejected as the *whole* story: outages unchanged, `NO_SIGNAL` fell anyway, and pre-auth failures tripled with no case created.
- **"Fewer complaints = fewer underlying problems"** → rejected: `service_events` in South stayed steady/high May–Jun.
- **"LEGACY and NOVA numbers are comparable"** → rejected: NOVA logs ~12× more sessions and excludes pre-auth from containment — different denominators.
- **"Transfers down proves efficiency"** → rejected: transfers are the route to agents/cases; their collapse plus pre-auth deaths is deflection, not resolution.

### 8c · Material unknowns (what would change the decision)

- **Exact containment metric definition** — precise numerator/denominator; is pre-auth *formally* excluded? (email says yes — get the spec).
- **Repeat-contact / callback rate** — do "quiet" South customers call back within 3–7 days? This is the **decisive CX signal** and it is **not in the data**.
- **What happens to a pre-auth failure** operationally — any callback, queued case, or SMS follow-up, or is it truly dropped?
- **Why case-creation collapsed** 49.5%→3.3% — a genuine rule change, or sessions dying before that stage?
- **Whether self-served issues were actually resolved** (no repeat) vs merely closed.
- **Is `subscriber_id` from caller-ID (ANI)?** If so, a "pre-auth" session may still be identifiable — affects how "lost" they are.

### 8d · Conclusion options (choose one — all defensible)

| **A** *(recommended)* | **Not evidence of CX improvement; do NOT expand.** Decline is mostly a measurement + auth artifact, with *some* genuine self-service. Fix auth, add all-contact + repeat-contact metrics, re-measure | You're convinced the artifact dominates |

### 8e · Safe next action (reversible)

- **Do not expand nationally yet**; keep the South pilot **running** (reversible) but instrument it before deciding.
- Add **pre-auth abandonment** and **all-contact containment** to the dashboard; **reconcile IVR sessions ↔ CareDesk cases** so nothing is invisible.
