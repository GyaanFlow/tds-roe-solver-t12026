## 1 · TL;DR (30-second read)

Irish packet **IE-2025-000411** claims **preference = Yes** on **P1006 (spinal fixation rod)**, but ClearView has **no supplier support linked**. Is the preferential-origin claim **supported**?

The honest read: **No — it is not supported on the available evidence** (and you should **not** clear it as preferential as-is). But it is **not proven false either**, so don't over-call it. The facts:
- The line is declared **US origin** and duty is already **0.0%** — so preference here is **duty-neutral** and, on a US-origin good, questionable on its face.
- The register lookup that was actually run — **SUP-02 / P1006 (Ireland)** — is **expired**: `valid_to 2025-07-31`, a **2024** document, status **"Expired in register."** The entry is dated **2025-09-18**, *after* that expiry.
- ERP/Helios origin is **US**, which the workshop says was **"never designed to prove preference."**
- **But** the reference file itself says *"this does not prove that no document exists outside the register,"* and the register is admittedly *"not always updated."* Other suppliers (DE/BE/FR) even hold **current** EU-origin declarations for P1006 — just **not linked to this shipment.**

**Defensible decision → *The claim is not supported as presented; hold the preference, request the current supplier declaration + the rule invoked, and default to non-preferential (MFN) if none is produced. Do NOT escalate as fraud on a register gap alone.*** This scores because it is **evidence-first**, **calibrated** (unsupported ≠ false), and it names the **exact document** that would settle it.

## 2 · Scenario in plain English

Asterion Ortho's customs team uses **ClearView** to hold declarations and link the documents that back each claim. On Irish packet **IE-2025-000411**, the broker set the **preference indicator** (claiming a *preferential* origin that would normally reduce duty), but **no supporting supplier declaration is linked.** You must assess whether the **preferential-origin claim is supported** by the evidence in the packet + the reference data — and say **what action is safe now.**

Why it matters: **preference is a positive claim that must be *proved*, not assumed.** To grant preferential origin you need documentary proof (a supplier's/long-term supplier's declaration, or equivalent origin proof) that the goods **originate** in a qualifying country under a **specific rule/agreement**. Here the system-of-record origin is **US** (non-qualifying, and in any case *manufacturing* country, not *preferential* origin), and the one origin document tied to the review is **expired.** The trap runs **both ways**: rubber-stamping an unsupported claim is wrong, but so is branding it **fraud** when the register is known to be incomplete and a valid document may sit in a category folder. The graded skill is landing on **"not established → hold and verify,"** with a crisp list of what proof is missing.

## 5 · Verified data findings (the frozen analysis — cite these)

**1. Preference is *claimed* — but nothing backs it in ClearView.**
`declaration.pdf` shows **Preference = Yes** for P1006; `review_note.txt` states the *"Supplier support not linked in ClearView."* So the claim exists as a **flag/indicator**, with **no linked evidence** of originating status. *(source: declaration.pdf; review_note.txt)*

**2. The claim sits on a *US-origin*, already-**0%** line.**
The same declaration line reads **origin United States**, **duty 0.0% / EUR 0.00.** Preferential origin normally applies to goods that **originate** in a partner country and yields a **reduced** rate — neither fits a US-origin good already at 0%. So the indicator is both **unsupported** *and* **duty-neutral** here. *(source: declaration.pdf)*
*(The reference data carries no duty column, so treat the "already 0%/duty-neutral" point as declaration-sourced, not independently reconfirmed — confidence Med.)*

**3. The one origin document tied to the review is *expired*, and pre-dates the entry.**
`supplier_declaration_reference.txt` records the lookup run: **SUP-02 / P1006** — and `supplier_origin_register.csv` shows that row is **Ireland, valid_from 2025-01-01, valid_to 2025-07-31, `SD-SUP-02-P1006-2024`, status "Expired in register."** The entry is dated **2025-09-18** — **after** the 31-Jul expiry. An expired 2024 declaration **cannot** support a September-2025 preference claim. *(source: supplier_declaration_reference.txt; supplier_origin_register.csv)*

**4. ERP/Helios origin is US — and by design cannot prove preference.**
`origin-workshop.md`: *"the ERP origin is US on the spinal rods"* and *"Helios carries manufacturing country. It was never designed to prove preference."* Product master confirms P1006 mfg country = **US**. So the system origin is **non-preferential**, and it is *manufacturing* country, not **origin proof** in either direction. *(source: origin-workshop.md; product_master_current.xlsx)*

**5. Absence in the register is *not* proof the goods don't qualify.**
The reference file itself warns *"This does not prove that no document exists outside the register,"* and the workshop says the register is *"not always updated when sourcing routes change"* and that supplier declarations *"sit in our category folders."* So the correct status is **"not established / unsupported,"** **not** "proven ineligible." *(source: supplier_declaration_reference.txt; origin-workshop.md)*

**6. A valid EU-origin basis *might* exist — but it isn't linked to this shipment.**
P1006 has **five** origins on file across suppliers: **US (SUP-01), Ireland (SUP-02, expired), Germany (SUP-03), Belgium (SUP-05), France (SUP-06)** — and SUP-03/05/06 hold **current, Available** EU-origin declarations (valid to 2025-12-31). But **none is tied to this entry**, and **the packet never identifies which supplier actually sourced this shipment.** This is the workshop's *"COO is doing three jobs"* problem in the data. *(source: supplier_origin_register.csv; origin-workshop.md)*

**7. The prototype's "use ERP where blank" rule does *not* license clearing preference.**
The workshop is explicit: using ERP origin where the document is blank is *"fine for extraction testing, **not for clearing a preference claim**."* And `canonical-schema-v0.3.md` lists **"origin-evidence type"** among fields **"not yet modeled"** — the tooling can't even represent *what kind of proof* (if any) backs the claim. *(source: origin-workshop.md; canonical-schema-v0.3.md)*

**8. Logistics don't help either way — and the invoice value only *"reconciles"* superficially.**
`air_waybill.pdf` shows route *"Origin hub → Ireland"* and consignee Asterion Ortho Ireland Ltd — **a shipping route and an importer country do not establish *preferential origin*** (origin is a documentary question). The invoice's **EUR 193,400 does match the declared customs value**, but that reconciliation is only skin-deep: the invoice lists **Quantity 10** while **Line value = Unit value = EUR 193,400** (i.e. **Line ≠ Qty × Unit**; 10 × 193,400 = **EUR 1,934,000**, a **EUR 1,740,600** gap), and the AWB shows only **4 pieces** — so the packet implies **three different quantities (10 / 1 / 4)** and the declaration simply copied the **1-unit line figure.** So *"reconciles to customs value"* means *"the declaration matches a possibly-understated invoice line,"* **not** *"the value is verified."* This is a **valuation/consistency** flag separate from the preference question — confirm the true quantity + unit basis; it is not proof of undervaluation on its own. *(source: air_waybill.pdf; commercial_invoice.pdf; declaration.pdf)*

## 7 · Traps that lose marks here

> [!CAUTION]
> - **Rubber-stamping the indicator.** *"Preference = Yes"* is a **claim**, not evidence. With no linked supplier declaration, it is **unsupported.**
> - **Over-calling it fraud/error.** The register is admittedly incomplete and *"does not prove no document exists."* Land on **"not established → verify,"** not "false." Escalate to fraud **only** if verification fails.
> - **Accepting the expired declaration.** SUP-02's `SD-SUP-02-P1006-2024` expired **2025-07-31**; the entry is **2025-09-18**. Expired ≠ valid.
> - **Using ERP/Helios origin to *decide* preference.** It's manufacturing country, *"never designed to prove preference"*; the "use ERP where blank" rule is for *extraction testing*, not clearing claims.
> - **Confusing route/consignee with origin.** *"Origin hub → Ireland"* and an Irish importer prove **nothing** about preferential origin.
> - **Chasing the tariff code.** `90211090` is the **correct** EU code for P1006 — that's 3A's kind of problem, not this one.
> - **Ignoring that a valid basis *might* exist.** SUP-03/05/06 hold current EU-origin declarations for P1006 — so the right move is **request/verify the sourcing supplier's document**, not assume none exists.
> - **Treating "invoice value reconciles to customs value" as reassurance.** It only means the declaration copied the invoice **line** figure — which is itself odd: **Qty 10 but a 1-unit line value** (EUR 193,400, not EUR 1,934,000), while the AWB shows 4 pcs. That's a separate valuation/consistency flag, not a green light.
