## 1 · TL;DR (30-second read)

Operations says routine QC release "takes about a day and a half" and wants a **dashboard by tomorrow** turning that into a performance KPI. Your job: **does anything smell wrong enough to verify first?**

**Yes — loudly.** The "cycle time" isn't safe to use as a KPI because the number depends entirely on **undefined choices**: (1) **which clock** — receipt→release is ~45 h, but the SOP target is measured **evidence-available→release** (~21 h median); (2) **a snapshot artifact** — **70.6 % of release timestamps are stamped at exactly `02:10:00`**, the QCore daily-snapshot time, so the intraday duration is fabricated for most rows; (3) the SOP says **complex exceptions are excluded** from the routine target, but ~28 % of rows are exceptions. Clean it up and the median falls to **~5–8 h**. Same data, answers from ~5 h to ~45 h. On top of those definitional choices sits a **survivorship trap**: the 42 open-HOLD batches are *entirely* the most recent week of receipts (all ≥ Jul 24), so any cycle time is measured only on older, already-cleared work — **blind to the current backlog** ($6.6M shortage exposure sitting in HOLD).

**Defensible verdict → *It smells; do NOT ship the KPI yet.*** **Smallest verification:** confirm what `qcore_release_ts` actually means (the data catalog + a live-QCore spot-check of the `02:10:00` batches). This scores well because it **catches a measurement trap before it becomes a target.**

## 2 · Scenario in plain English

You are in **Quality Systems** at a consumer-products maker. **Operations** wants a dashboard, live *tomorrow*, that reports QC-release **cycle time** as a performance KPI — on the strength of a claim that release "takes about a day and a half."

Why it matters: a cycle-time number that becomes a **KPI** drives staffing, SLAs, and pressure on reviewers. If the number is built on the wrong timestamps or mixes in work the process **explicitly excludes**, you'll be managing an artifact — and once it's a target, people optimize the artifact. You have the **QC release extract**, the **written disposition process (SOP)**, and a **freshness note** telling you how stale each upstream snapshot is. The task is a *smell test*: not "build the metric," but "**should we trust this enough to build on, and if not, what is the single cheapest check?**"

## 5 · Verified data findings (the frozen analysis — cite these)

**1. The clock is undefined — and it swings the answer ~6×.**
Receipt→release: **mean 45.5 h / median 39.4 h.** Evidence(lab)→release (the SOP's own definition): **mean 26.9 h / median 21.3 h.** "About a day and a half" (~36 h) only matches the *receipt* clock — which is **not** the SOP metric. You cannot ship a KPI without pinning the definition. *(source: `batch_release.csv` + `qc_release_sop.md`)*

**2. The biggest smell: 70.6 % of release times are the daily-snapshot boundary.**
**185 of 262** `qcore_release_ts` values are stamped at **exactly `02:10:00`** — the QCore daily refresh time (`source_snapshot_ts` = `2026-07-31T02:10:00`, QCore = "daily"). (Restricted to the **220 rows that carry *any* release time**, that's **84.1 %** — the 70.6 % counts the 42 open-HOLD rows with no release time in the denominator; either way, most real release times are the boundary.) QCore records the release **date** and defaults the **time** to its `02:10` batch boundary, so the intraday duration is **fabricated** for most rows. Drop these and evidence→release falls from mean 26.9 h → **19.4 h**, median 21.3 h → **8.4 h**. *(source: `batch_release.csv` + `source_freshness.csv`)*

**3. Complex exceptions are included — the SOP says exclude them.**
**73 rows (27.9 %)** are exceptions: 31 `open_deviation=true`, 13 `market_spec_override=true`, 39 `coa_status≠AVAILABLE`. The SOP excludes complex exceptions from the routine 24 h target while open. Restrict to **routine + non-artifact** rows and you get **48 rows — but 25 of those are still-open HOLD with a null `qcore_release_ts`** (no cycle time exists yet), leaving **23 rows that actually have a cycle time.** Those 23 average **mean 7.8 h, median 5.5 h, and 22/23 = 95.7 % land within 24 h.** So the *genuine* routine process is **fast and comfortably inside the 24 h target** — the exact opposite of the ~45 h receipt-clock headline, which is inflated by the wrong clock, the `02:10` artifact, and unfinished HOLD work. **(Watch the denominator: 22/48 = 45.8 % is wrong — 25 of those 48 have no cycle time to be "within 24 h" in the first place; the honest base is the 23 released rows.)** *(source: `batch_release.csv` + `qc_release_sop.md`)*

**4. Scope is undefined: which dispositions even have a cycle time?**
Of 262 rows: **215 RELEASED, 42 HOLD, 4 REJECTED, 1 CONDITIONAL.** The 42 HOLD rows have a **null `qcore_release_ts`** (still open — no cycle time exists yet). A KPI that quietly drops or mishandles open items reports only the *finished* work and flatters itself. *(source: `batch_release.csv`)*

**5. "COA missing" is probably index lag, not a control failure — but either way it's a smell.**
**17** batches are RELEASED with `coa_status=MISSING` and **13** with `INDEX_PENDING`. The SOP requires cert evidence before disposition, yet `source_freshness` calls CertVault a "nightly index" — so `INDEX_PENDING/MISSING` most likely reflects **snapshot lag**, not truly absent certs. That means the extract's status fields are **point-in-time stale**, which is exactly why you verify before trusting it. *(source: `batch_release.csv` + `source_freshness.csv` + `qc_release_sop.md`)*

**6. The arithmetic is clean — the problem is *semantics*, not bugs.**
**Zero** negative durations, **zero** lab-complete-before-receipt, no null receipt/lab timestamps. So this isn't garbage math you can filter away — it's that the timestamps **don't mean what the KPI assumes.** That is the essence of the smell. *(source: `batch_release.csv`)*

**7. HOLD is a *receipt-date artifact*, not a quality signal — so the KPI is right-censored.**
Every one of the **42 HOLD** rows was received **on or after 2026-07-24**; every one of the **220 dispositioned** rows (215 RELEASED + 4 REJECTED + 1 CONDITIONAL) was received **on or before 2026-07-20** — with **zero receipts in the 21–23 Jul gap**, and the whole extract snapshotted at **2026-07-31 02:10**. So "HOLD" here means *"arrived in the last week and the 07-31 snapshot hasn't dispositioned it yet"* — not a quality problem. The **latest release timestamp is 2026-07-22**, nine days before the snapshot, so the newest cohort is entirely un-released. Two consequences: **(a)** any cycle time is computed **only on older survivors**, never on the current week; **(b)** the most recent, most decision-relevant batches — **$5.97M inventory (16.1 %) and $6.64M shortage-exposure (20.1 %)** — are **invisible** to the metric (null release → no duration to average). A dashboard built on this will look fast and stable precisely because it **can't see the live backlog.** *(source: `batch_release.csv` — receipt_ts vs disposition)*

## 6 · Claim vs. Reality

| The claim / assumption | What the data actually shows | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| "Release takes about a day and a half" | Only true on the **receipt→release** clock (~45 h). SOP clock (evidence→release) median is ~21 h; cleaned, ~5–8 h | Depends on undefined choices |
| `qcore_release_ts` = the moment of disposition | 70.6 % are the `02:10` snapshot boundary — a **date**, not an intraday time | Mostly artifact |
| The extract = ready to become a KPI | Clock, scope, and exception rules are all undefined; status fields are snapshot-stale | Not KPI-ready |
| "COA MISSING" = released without a certificate | More likely CertVault **index lag** (nightly), i.e. a freshness artifact | Unproven — verify |
| The data is broken/garbage | Arithmetic is clean; the issue is **timestamp semantics**, not integrity | Reframe, don't discard |
| The queue is current / QC is keeping up | The newest week of receipts (42 batches, **$6.6M shortage exposure**) is **100 % un-dispositioned HOLD**; cycle time is measured only on older survivors | Right-censored — blind to the backlog |

## 7 · Traps that lose marks here

> [!CAUTION]
> - **Averaging `qcore_release_ts − receipt_ts` and calling it "cycle time."** That's the wrong clock (SOP measures from *evidence available*) **and** 70 % of the release times are a snapshot artifact.
> - **Trusting the `02:10:00` timestamps as real event times.** They're the daily batch boundary — you only know the release *date*.
> - **Including HOLD/exception rows** in a "routine" cycle-time KPI when the SOP excludes them.
> - **Measuring cycle time only on rows that have a release timestamp.** The 42 HOLD rows (all received in the last week, ≥ Jul 24) have none — so the metric silently drops the *entire current backlog* and reports only older, already-cleared work (survivorship).
> - **Reporting a single mean** on a skewed, contaminated distribution (mean 26.9 h vs median 21.3 h vs cleaned 5.5 h).
> - **Calling "COA MISSING" a compliance breach** without checking it isn't just CertVault index lag.
> - **Over-verifying.** The question asks for the *smallest* check — name one decisive, cheap check, not a data-quality program.
> - **Answering "no, it's fine."** Defensible only if you engage the `02:10` artifact and the clock definition — and the data makes that hard.

### 8b · Hypotheses considered and rejected (with the evidence)

- **"1.5 days is a real, trustworthy cycle time."** → rejected: it only appears on the non-SOP receipt clock, and 70 % of release times are a snapshot artifact.
- **"The data is broken — throw it out."** → rejected: arithmetic is clean (no negatives, no nulls in receipt/lab); the issue is timestamp *meaning*, not corruption. Verify, don't discard.
- **"COA MISSING proves batches were released without certificates."** → rejected (as proven): more consistent with CertVault nightly-index lag; needs a live-portal check before alleging a control breach.
- **"Just report the mean."** → rejected: distribution is skewed and contaminated; a single mean hides the artifact.

### 8c · What would change the decision (verify / ask for next)

- **The data catalog definition of `qcore_release_ts`** (and `lab_complete_ts`) — does `02:10:00` mean "released this day" or a real disposition time? *(decisive)*
- **A live-QCore spot-check** of ~5–10 `02:10:00` batches: do their true disposition times vary? If yes → the extract time is a snapshot artifact.
- **The agreed metric definition**: endpoints (evidence→disposition), which dispositions count, and explicit exclusion of open exceptions.
- **A fresher, aligned extract** (or event-level QCore log) so LabTrack/QCore lags don't contaminate the delta.

### 8d · Conclusion options (choose one — all defensible)

| Option | Stance | When to pick it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **A** *(recommended)* | **It smells — don't ship the KPI yet.** Timestamps/scope/exclusions undefined; one cheap check (catalog + live-QCore spot-check of `02:10` rows) decides it | You engage the `02:10` artifact head-on |
| **B** | **Provisional KPI with caveats** — publish the *routine, non-artifact* median (~5–8 h) clearly labelled, pending the catalog check | You want to give Ops something usable now |
| **C** | **Insufficient basis to measure** — the metric definition itself is missing; define it before any dashboard | You want maximum caution |

### 8e · Smallest verification (the reversible next step)

- **Do not** publish the dashboard from this extract as-is.
- **Smallest decisive check:** pull the **true disposition timestamp from live QCore** (system of record) for a handful of batches whose `qcore_release_ts = 02:10:00`. If those times vary in QCore but are all `02:10` in the extract, the extract time is a **snapshot default** → the cycle-time KPI is invalid until the timestamp is redefined.
- Then pin the **metric definition** (evidence→disposition, RELEASED only, exclude open exceptions) before building anything.
